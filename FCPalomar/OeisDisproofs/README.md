# Counterexamples to five OEIS conjectures (A333564, A034171, A298799, A382407, A399793)

Five conjectures from OEIS comments are stated and refuted, each by an explicit witness that Lean's kernel evaluates. (1) Peter Bala (December 2019) conjectured that c(pn) ≡ c(n) (mod p²) for c(n) = A034171(n − 1) = 3^(n−1) Π_{k<n} (3k + 1)/n! and primes p ≡ 1 (mod 3), and for the coefficients a(n) of (1 − 27x)^(−1/9) (A298799) and primes p ≡ 1 (mod 9), for all n ≥ 1 outside an explicit set of n = mp + k with 0 ≤ m ≤ (p − 1)/d and 1 ≤ k ≤ (p − 1)/d (d = 3 or 9). Every exception lies below p². The package shows that both congruences fail at n = p² + 1: for A034171 at p = 7, n = 50, and for A298799 at p = 19, n = 362, where a(6878) has 9841 digits. So the exception sets are incomplete. (Outside the Lean, a search over n < 400 finds n = p² + 1 as the first failure for A034171 at p = 7, 13 and 19.) The package also proves that (1 − 27x)^(−1/9) is the unique power series over ℚ with constant term 1 and A^9(1 − 27x) = 1. (2) Bala's general clause on A333564 (April 2020) says that a(r,s;n) = 2^(rn) Σ_{j<sn} (−1)^(sn−1+j) C(n+j,j)^r satisfies a(np^k) ≡ a(np^(k−1)) (mod p^(3k)), with the prime range p ≥ 3 read from the entry's revision history. It fails at (r,s,p,n,k) = (2,1,3,1,1). The entry's own case r = s = 1 is not refuted. (3) Felix Huber's conjecture that a(k) = 0 exactly for k in {2, 4, 8, 13} ∪ {3^j} ∪ {5·3^j} (A382407) fails at k = 10; the package also proves a(k) = 0 for every k in the amended set, by a mod 3 descent adapted from Huang and Sun. (4) The conjecture on A399793 that the largest bad subset of {1, …, n} has the size ℓ(n) of the entry's lower bound for n ≥ 13 fails for 13 ≤ n ≤ 17, by explicit bad sets of size ℓ(n) + 1. The failures in (3) and (4) are visible in the entries' own data, and (4) was noted publicly in September 2026. A399793 concerns balanced signings of sets of integers, a topic studied for dense sets by Károlyi (Integers, 2009); the entry records equality for n ≥ 564 and links B. Wang's note on large n. The audience is number theorists who study Dwork- and Lucas-type congruences for hypergeometric terms and Bala's congruence conjectures, several of which have been proved in recent notes (for example Kallat, arXiv:2607.18313 and arXiv:2609.07238), and combinatorial number theorists who work on balanced subset sums. Searches in November 2026 found no earlier refutation of (1) or (2).
