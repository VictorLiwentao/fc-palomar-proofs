# OEIS A291897 and A292911: Shevelev's divisibility conjectures for Euler polynomials

Let E_m(x) be the Euler polynomials, Σ E_m(x)t^m/m! = 2e^(xt)/(e^t + 1), and E_k the Euler numbers, sech t = Σ E_k t^k/k!. OEIS A291897 is the numerator of E_(2n−1)(n), and A292911 is the set of n ≥ 1 with (2n − 1)³ dividing it. In September 2017 Vladimir Shevelev conjectured that (2n − 1)² divides A291897(n) for every n ≥ 1, and that every prime p ≡ 1 (mod 4) has the form 2a − 1 with a in A292911. The package proves both conjectures and their sharp forms: (2n − 1)³ divides A291897(n) if and only if 2n − 1 divides E_(2n−2), and, for an odd prime p, (p + 1)/2 lies in A292911 if and only if p ≡ 1 (mod 4). The Lean definitions of E_m(x) and E_k are recurrences, and the package checks them against the two e.g.f.s. With N = 2n − 1, the classical expansion of E_m(x) about 1/2 (DLMF 24.2.10) at x = n gives the first result and the criterion; the prime case then follows from Ely's congruence (1882): E_(p−1) ≡ 0 or 2 (mod p) as p ≡ 1 or 3 (mod 4). The package proves both ingredients in Lean. By the sharp form, the numbers 2a − 1 with a in A292911 are exactly the odd m with m | E_(m−1). The first composite one is 1105 = 5·13·17, at a = 553. Thomas Ordowski conjectured (OEIS A122045, 2020) that every odd composite m with m | E_(m−1) is a Carmichael number whose prime factors are all ≡ 1 (mod 4). The package does not treat that conjecture, or Shevelev's companion conjecture A292782. The audience is number theorists who study congruences of Euler numbers and Euler polynomials modulo primes and prime powers. No earlier proof of Shevelev's conjectures was found.
